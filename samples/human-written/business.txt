Running a Coffee Shop: What Nobody Tells You

I opened my coffee shop two years ago. Thought it'd be chill—make coffee, chat with customers, maybe read a book during slow hours. Reality check: I work harder now than I did at my office job.

The money thing caught me off guard. Rent is obvious, but there's a thousand small costs that add up. Napkins. Bags. The little stir sticks. Credit card processing fees take like 3% of every sale. My espresso machine needed repairs twice in the first year. Each time was $800 minimum.

Staffing is the real headache though. You think you'll hire people who care about coffee. Mostly you get people who need a paycheck and see this as just another job. Nothing wrong with that, but it means you're always training. Turnover in food service is brutal. Lost three baristas in six months last year.

Regular customers make it worth it. There's this older guy, Warren, who comes in every morning at 6:30. Orders the same thing. Small black coffee. Sits by the window for an hour reading his tablet. Never tips. But he's consistent, you know? Consistency matters a lot when you're trying to predict how much milk to order.

The hours are rough too. I'm here at 5am most days. Getting up that early never gets easier. My social life basically died. Friends don't understand why I can't just close early or take a random Tuesday off. The shop's open seven days a week, and if I'm not here, things go wrong.
